Introduction
  • There is no known Russian film that depicts or is titled as a “Russian military child rape film.” The search results do not identify any such movie produced by the Russian film industry with that specific theme or title.
  • However, several films and real-life incidents involve disturbing themes of sexual violence related to Russian military contexts:
1. Cargo 200 (2007)
  • Cargo 200 is a controversial Russian drama directed by Aleksey Balabanov, set in 1984 during the Soviet-Afghan War. It includes graphic scenes of rape, murder, and psychological abuse, including a scene where a young woman is raped with a vodka bottle by a Soviet officer. While it does not involve child rape, its brutal depiction of sexual violence caused significant backlash in Russia.
2. A Woman in Berlin (2008)
  • This German-Russian co-production is based on an anonymous diary from 1945 and depicts the mass rape of German women by Soviet soldiers during the final days of World War II. While it includes harrowing scenes of sexual violence, it does not focus on child rape.
3. Real-Life Allegations (Not Films)
  • In 2022, during the Russia-Ukraine war, a Russian soldier named Alexei Bychkov was arrested after a video surfaced allegedly showing him sexually abusing a baby. This incident was widely reported in international media but is not the subject of a film.
Clarification
  • If you are looking for a film that specifically portrays child rape by Russian military personnel, no such feature film currently exists in public databases or credible sources. Some films address wartime sexual violence broadly, but none focus explicitly on child rape by Russian soldiers as a central plot.
